## Evidence-supported correspondence

Research describes flow as deep task absorption that is more likely when task challenge and personal skill correspond. Clear goals and unambiguous feedback are also repeatedly studied as flow conditions.

Research on cognitive control distinguishes:

- **cognitive stability** — maintaining focus on task-relevant information;
- **cognitive flexibility** — shifting goals, rules, or attention when environmental conditions change.

Research also distinguishes internally directed and externally directed attention. Strong resistance to external interference can support focused learning while reducing flexibility when the environment requires a changed response.

Embodied and embedded approaches describe cognition as interaction among brain, body, activity, social setting, and environment rather than as an isolated internal store.

The evidence therefore supports this bounded correspondence:

~~~text
INTERNAL GOAL / INTENT
-> TASK-RELEVANT FOCUS
-> ACTION IN AN ENVIRONMENT
-> EXTERNAL RESPONSE
-> FEEDBACK
-> INTERNAL UPDATE OR PRESERVED COURSE
-> CONTINUED ACTION, CORRECTION, OR PAUSE
~~~

## What connects the sides

The missing connector is **feedback**.

Intent provides an internal direction. The environment returns observable conditions and consequences. Feedback reveals whether the present course still corresponds or requires correction.

~~~text
INTENT
-> ACTION
-> ENVIRONMENTAL RESPONSE
-> FEEDBACK
-> ASSESSMENT
-> CORRECTION OR CONTINUATION
-> VERIFIED RETURN
~~~

Feedback does not transfer identity, create authority, or prove complete mutual understanding. It makes the relationship inspectable.
